## Reference

Use visual reference. Preserve the exact compact alien Mars bug: horizontal oval body, head and curved mandibles on the left, rust-orange segmented chitin, dark-brown joints, smooth painted rendering and fixed top-down three-quarter game view.

## Prompt

Locked sprite animation: keep the input image camera, framing, angle, scale and orientation absolutely unchanged; animate only the existing legs in place.

This is an image-to-video animation task. Treat the reference as a locked 2D game sprite, not a 3D scene or character turnaround. Generate a 3-second seamless walking-cycle reference video of the exact same bug. Do not redesign it or turn it into a spider, scorpion or different insect.

Animate a natural alternating-tripod walk in place. Tripod A is left-front, right-middle and left-rear; tripod B is right-front, left-middle and right-rear. One tripod lifts and swings forward while the other stays planted; land the feet before smoothly switching support. Articulate each leg at its joints, with slight timing offsets, short steps and subtle weight shifts. Never move all legs on one side together or lift all six at once. No rigid swings, foot sliding, skating, floating or snapping. Keep the body, direction, camera and framing fixed; animate only the six existing legs in the image plane.

Use smooth full-colour modern 2.5D sci-fi RTS rendering with anti-aliased edges, matte rust-brown chitin, restrained orange highlights, stable neutral lighting and consistent materials. No motion blur, flicker or morphing. Leg articulation must remain visible at small game scale.

Keep the bug centered at fixed scale and pivot; never reframe to follow a leg. First and final poses match for looping; use the reference image for both. Keep the complete head, both mandibles, all legs and shadow visible and unchanged in every frame. Nothing may disappear, deform, crop or touch an edge. Maintain at least 18% empty margin around the full moving silhouette; it occupies 50–55% of the square frame height. Do not shrink the bug during animation.

Add one soft, compact, dark neutral-brown contact shadow directly beneath the bug, centered on the bug's vertical axis with no directional offset. The light source is directly overhead, so the shadow is uniform and evenly distributed around the lower footprint of the body and legs, with no cast direction. The shadow must move consistently with the bug, remain fully inside the frame and stay within 10% of the bug's footprint. Shadow opacity 35–50%, feathered edge, no hard outline.

## Background and settings

Use a perfectly uniform saturated chroma-green background, approximately `#00FF00`, with no texture, floor, horizon, gradient, dust, smoke, particles or extra objects. The green must not appear in the bug or shadow. If true alpha is supported, preserve the bug and shadow with alpha; otherwise keep the flat green background for later keying.

Settings: 3 seconds; static camera; fixed focal length; fixed center and scale; no camera shake, zoom, pan or motion blur. Motion begins and ends in the same neutral pose. Keep at least three legs visibly planted in every phase. Feet in the support group remain planted and do not slide while the opposite tripod swings. Avoid synchronized stiff pendulum motion: each leg bends at its joints and the front, middle and rear feet move with slight natural timing offsets. Each swing leg returns to a clear ground contact before support transfers.

## Negative prompt
